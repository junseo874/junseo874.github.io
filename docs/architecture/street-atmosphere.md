# Street atmosphere: engine comparison and web implementation

Reference recordings supplied 2026-10-26: `2026-10-26 04-46-09.mp4` (12.5 seconds) and `2026-10-26 04-22-45.mp4` (11.416 seconds). Both show Unity's OutSide scene: elevator → street → shops. Inspected four frames per video across the movement. The pools reflect the fence, poles and lit background; they do not translate with the player. Rings are short lived and flattened vertically. Distant buildings receive more haze than the opaque shops. Bloom spreads around signs and bright gaps, without blurring all sprite edges.

## Authoritative settings

Read-only importer: `bar-playtest/tools/import-street-atmosphere.cjs [--write] [--engine=...]`. It exports only environment settings to `data/presentation/street-atmosphere.js`; no engine assets or source art are modified. Source hashes record the scene, volume and shaders. Default shader values differ substantially from the actual scene, so use scene overrides:

- Nine pools, four procedural outlines; their world coordinates and sizes come from `OutSide.unity`.
- Reflection horizon −0.86, compression 0.667, opacity 0.525, 90 PPU, irregular edge strength 1, distortion 0.7 pixels, ripple brightness 0.45, radius 0.15 units. Pool 3 emits 3 impacts/sec; the others emit 5. Sixteen slots, lifetime 1.25 seconds; no accumulated surface history.
- `SH_OutsidePuddle`: quantized native-pixel mask; elliptical expanding rings with y-distance ×2.7; secondary rings, trough and brief impact flash; four vertically averaged reflection taps; reflection coordinates clamped above the wall baseline; screen-edge fade. Foreground poles/rails/cabin front are added to the reflection capture separately, before they later occlude the player normally.
- Three `BackgroundFog` components: density 0.70/0.55/0.35, height 7/6/4.5, falloff 0.7/0.8/1. Ground −0.9. Two drifting value-noise octaves, scale 2.5, strength 0.45, speed 0.12, PPU 100. Horizontal span follows each authored target and its current parallax position.
- One `LightShaft`: mesh from the authored Light2D shape, length 1.15, width 0.9, intensity 0.3. Ported edge/body fade, gently moving rays and small flicker; the engine dust grid is intentionally omitted. It is captured in puddles.
- `VP_OutSide`: Bloom threshold 0.75, intensity 1.4, scatter 0.75. Chromatic aberration, split toning, tone mapping and shadow/midtone grading are inactive. Do not enable them merely because their serialized values exist. Window rain runs **after** postprocessing in `WindowRainFeature`.

## Web rendering contract

`street-atmosphere-renderer.js` owns reusable WebGL2 programs/textures. `street-atmosphere.js` owns bounded per-model impacts and render ordering. Integration is limited to three calls in `outside.js`. The old web layer indices are preserved: fog enters at 2/3/4, ground surfaces at 9 before actors, foreground still covers actors normally. The player’s current animation is projected into the reflection capture at its visible foot plane. Interaction outlines and DOM speech UI are not captured. The final bloom precedes lens droplets, discovery markers and transition fades. The previous arbitrary top/bottom screen-darkening gradient is retained indoors only; the engine street volume has no active vignette.

Reflection/fog compositing and bloom operate in linear color, then return to sRGB. Presentation matches the physical canvas dimensions on resize/DPR changes. Shader masks stay on world/native-pixel grids. Paused/repeated draws do not advance impacts; rain stopping lets existing rings expire, and a restarted scene clock resets them. Persistent pools match the engine even when rain is off.

### Deliberate browser adaptations

This is a shader-equation port, not a claim of bit-identical URP rendering. Canvas scene color is LDR: Unity HDR Light2D buffers cannot be recovered from PNGs. Existing authored sign/light overlays remain; bloom uses a bounded separable multiscale blur with the exported threshold/gain/scatter. The original scene remains sharp. Fog's unavailable ShapeLight texture is approximated by restrained local scene-light pickup. The shaft keeps the soft glow and slow ray modulation but intentionally omits the engine’s falling square dust grid: rain is rendered only by the existing weather-driven `outside-rain.js` streak field, avoiding a second, slower fall direction and bright debris-like dots. Exported dust settings remain as source-reference metadata and are not consumed by the web renderer. The shaft uses the same four mesh corners; its small subdivision/perspective differences are immaterial in an orthographic view. A deterministic browser hash seeds impacts (same distribution and rates, not the identical .NET random sequence). Existing lens-water simulation is unchanged.

CharacterSpriteLighting was originally inspected alongside the ground-shadow system; its body/rim pass is now ported separately as described below. The player reflection reuses the current shaded sprite frame. Existing street light-cookie/wall-light and CRT/fan/sign animation ports are retained.

## Character rim light (2026-10-27)

Reference is the locally available engine branch `65-liquid-pixel-shading`, commit `654756621b39611bbca64d3ad70cff8e5b6613e8` (not the older checked-out `develop`). Inspected `OutSide.unity`, `OutsideGround/CharacterSpriteLighting.cs`, `OutsideShadowLight.cs` and `SH_CharacterSpriteLit.shader` read-only. Scene components cover Luna, CitizenA/B, Samho, shiba and Bubi. The web applies this family to exterior player/NPC sprites and the replaceable outdoor Boobi dummy; home and QA resource previews retain their existing artwork. Engine files are unchanged.

`effects/character-lighting.js` owns the extracted settings and per-actor state. It shares the current world-space light inventory, flicker gain and moving-elevator transform with `character-shadows.js`, but follows the engine's **single strongest light with 15% hysteresis** for the body rim (ground shadows still use multiple lights). Strength moves toward influence / 0.6 over 0.3 seconds, retaining the last light direction while fading out. Same-frame reflection/body rendering, pause and restart do not advance it twice. Neighborhood actor IDs stay stable while walking.

`street-atmosphere-renderer.js` renders only the original native sprite cell, without another context or a full-screen scene upload. It ports directional alpha-neighbor edges, opposite-side shade, squared foot occlusion, light-color mixing (75%), rim width 1, intensity 1.2, direction focus 2, distance falloff 1.5 and glow band 0.6 / 3 texels. Flip changes texture-space light direction, not the world light. Sampling is clamped to the current frame, so adjacent atlas frames cannot appear in the outline. Textures are cached with a 64-image bound. The original alpha, animation, pivot and foreground occlusion remain intact; interaction outlines are a separate pass. Player puddle reflections reuse the shading and restore the shared GPU pass afterward.

Browser adaptation: the existing Canvas/LDR base color remains authoritative rather than inventing Unity's unavailable URP ShapeLight buffer (`receive2DLights`). Body shade and rim are computed in linear color and passed into the existing bloom, but HDR highlights above the browser's LDR range are clipped; this is not a claim of bit-identical Unity HDR output. No extra blur is applied to the character art.

They cover light selection, hysteresis, fade/pause/reset, opposite light directions and facing reversal, atlas/alpha boundaries, repeat rendering, street/corridor/elevator and resizing. Actual exterior screenshots are written to `/tmp/character-rim-*.png` during the browser test.

The 57 exterior checks passed including a separate rerun of Samho's test after adapting its draw interception to the shaded canvas (same source-cell assertions, no relaxed animation expectations). The UI suite passed 26/27 including a successful standalone QA-menu retry after a timeout; the previously documented elevator landing-input failure remains outside this change. ## Movement stability and player reflection follow-up

Reproduced a bloom sampling defect with a stationary 3-pixel white light translated in one-pixel steps: the old quarter-resolution single-tap extractor returned alternating energies `[0, 0, 2980, 2980, 0, 0, 2980, 2980]`. This was light undersampling, not a fog simulation reset. Thresholding/linear-color extraction now covers the 4×4 source footprint before reduction; target dimensions track physical viewport size. Intermediate bloom buffers use RGBA16F where supported (8-bit fallback), avoiding repeated low-energy quantization/banding. Blur radii retain the same logical-screen spread on resize. Tests at widths 800/1280/1920 in both directions measured under 0.05% energy variation on the float path, with no dropped light.

Player reflections reuse the same idle/walk/run frame index and facing as the visible actor. The idle sprite’s cached opaque bounds define the stable sole baseline (rather than the transparent cell bottom); run-frame foot lifts do not move the water plane. Capture reprojection foreshortens the actor by 0.22, at 0.82 capture opacity, then applies the existing water tint, mask and ripple displacement. Foreground poles/rails are painted after the reflected actor, preserving occlusion. Capture is refreshed per distinct reflection plane; upstairs and travelling elevator passengers are excluded. Elevator foreground reflection now uses the actual `elevatorY` property.

`test-street-atmosphere-motion-ui.cjs` covers moving horizontal/vertical light sources at three sizes; live player idle/walk/run/turn, deterministic paused frames, pool-boundary clipping, and upstairs/elevator exclusion. Real game screenshots also compare idle, walking and running over a pool. This test is in the `outside` suite.

Follow-up validation: smoke 16/16 and outside 49/49 passed. Three motion-test viewport sizes, real idle/walk/run captures and both sides of the joined pool were inspected; no pool-mask leaks or GPU errors. Structure/site and the engine settings snapshot still pass.

## Player shadows from engine lighting

The original `tools/import-character-shadows.cjs` extraction captured the OutSide player component, ten `OutsideShadowLight` emitters, three `OutsideGroundReceiver` regions and shadow material from the earlier engine checkout. `data/presentation/character-shadows.js` records source hashes and scene overrides. No engine or source art is changed. Freeform emitters use the two highest shape points, not their object pivot. Source enabled state, player sorting-layer membership, intensity, luminance, radius and priority all participate in influence; existing web neon outage gains are shared by light ID.

`character-shadows.js` now evaluates all ten emitter anchors simultaneously, with stable per-light identities and time-based energy smoothing. Projection slope, 0.28 flattening, 0.85 maximum length, foot anchor and animated sprite alpha retain the engine tuning. Atmospheric area sources have softer edges than lamps, and penumbra softness grows away from the feet. State belongs to the model clock: pause does not advance it and a restart clears old energy. Idle/walk/run and facing reuse the exact current actor atlas frame and snapped pivot.

`tools/import-shadow-lighting.cjs` independently audits the **current** `OutSide.unity`: 89 Light2Ds, consisting of 58 freeforms, 30 sprite cookies and one global light. Ten preserve the previously imported casting anchors; 48 further player-targeting freeform volumes provide local fill; the global light supplies ambient fill. Sprite cookies/background decoration are not counted a second time as independent emitters. The earlier engine shadow components are no longer present in the current local engine checkout, so their foot/receiver tuning remains in the prior `character-shadows.js` snapshot; this new importer refreshes only current lighting, records its own scene hash and does not fabricate replacement C# provenance.

At each floor pixel the GPU sums incident caster energy and the energy blocked by each projected silhouette. Shadow contrast is `blocked / (ambient + all incident caster energy + local fill)`. Consequently an opposite lamp makes its own shadow but also reduces the contrast of an unblocked shadow; overlaps cannot accumulate as independent opaque black copies. Local fill is sampled from the actual freeform polygons and their falloff, not from a whole-screen brightness guess. A 0.05-world-unit grid covers only the player's reachable shadow footprint, with 16-bit packed irradiance and bilinear sampling. The grid is anchored in world coordinates. Existing neon outage IDs drive both caster/fill gains; storefront fields follow the same art offset as their visible cookies and cabin lights/fields follow the moving elevator.

The reusable atmosphere WebGL renderer clips to the receiving floor, reuses five-tap alpha softness and distance fade, and converts the shadow material color to linear space. Contact and projected shadows share one pass after puddles and before actors/foreground, so shadows darken water while railings/cabin fronts still cover them. The street, corridor and moving cabin have clipped receiver rectangles; the latter two account for the web’s residential floor height and cabin art scale. Home and QA views retain their current presentation.

This is a bounded 2D lighting approximation using the authored scene layers and floor receivers, not a 3D ray-traced solution. Global ambient is scaled to 0.3 for the already-lit LDR pixel-art scene. It does not infer new foreground/back-wall geometry or wall occlusion from image brightness.

`test-character-shadows.cjs` covers projection direction/length, inactive/out-of-range lights, simultaneous emitters, polygon fill, pause/reset, corridor and moving cabin coordinates. `test-character-shadows-ui.cjs` verifies actual GPU output: opposite lights create two simultaneous shadows, a weaker source casts a weaker shadow, localized fill reduces only the illuminated side, no bright cutouts or floor-boundary leaks occur, current animation/facing changes the silhouette, and paused frames remain identical. It also renders the real street player, upstairs, in the lift and after resize.

Shadow follow-up validation: smoke 16/16 and outside 51/51 passed; structure/site checks and the read-only shadow snapshot comparison passed. Real street, corridor and moving-cabin captures were inspected.

Multi-light follow-up: smoke 16/16 and outside 51/51 passed. GPU measurements showed two simultaneous opposing shadows; weakening one emitter reduced its side from 26,955 to 7,293 accumulated darkening units. Local fill reduced that side to 6,596 while the unlit side stayed at 24,960. Floor-boundary leaks and brightened pixels were zero. Local draw-submission medians were 2.2 ms with shadows versus 1.9 ms without (CPU timing only). Current lighting snapshot, structure and site checks passed.

## Exterior rendering budget (2026-10-26)

The previous submission-only CPU samples did not measure GPU completion. A new audit uses `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`, discards unavailable/disjoint results, and reports CPU submission and rAF intervals separately. Run `node bar-playtest/tools/profile-outside-rendering.cjs [URL] [DPR]` against the local server. `BASELINE_EFFECTS_DIR` can serve saved pre-change `street-atmosphere-renderer.js` / `character-shadows.js` without changing the working tree. This is an opt-in diagnostic, not a device-dependent CI time limit.

On local headless Chrome / Apple M4 Pro (Metal), a rainy street at x=-2, 1280×720 CSS / DPR 2 (2560×1440 physical), two alternating before/after rounds each sampled 60 frames after warm-up. Sum of the measured pass means (fog ×3 plus shafts/puddles/shadow/bloom/rain maps/rain composite) fell from approximately **9.7 ms to 6.2 ms**, about **36%**. CPU submission medians remained roughly **2.2–2.5 ms**; the experiment does not establish a per-frame CPU improvement. At DPR 1, measured pass means fell from about 4.1 ms to 2.5 ms. These are effect-pass timings, not total browser GPU utilization, OS CPU percentages, battery consumption or a cross-device FPS promise. The test machine held approximately 60 rAF callbacks/sec before and after.

Changes preserve the full physical scene resolution and the original lighting equations:

- Fog noise/density is computed once per authored 100-PPU world cell into a bounded grid, then composited at full device resolution. Grid origin follows integer world cells. Very wide views use the original direct shader when a native-grid intermediate would cost more than it saves. Three moving-camera comparisons at DPR 2 differ from direct evaluation by at most one 8-bit channel level (half-float intermediate rounding), with mean difference about 0.012. A matched rainy-street screenshot at DPR 2 also differed by at most one channel level (mean about 0.0022), with no channel differing by more than three levels.
- Puddles, shafts and shadows use conservative device-pixel scissors and copy back only their affected rectangles. Off-screen pools/shafts skip both scene upload and reflection capture. Pool rectangles are cleared together and composited once, preserving overlaps and keeping one clean reflection capture per plane.
- Uniform locations are cached; two redundant identity bloom blur/copy passes are removed. Bloom’s complete 4×4 source footprint and float intermediate precision remain intact. The default framebuffer no longer requests preservation between frames; every presented region is explicitly overwritten/cleared before copying it synchronously to Canvas2D.
- Shadow fill coverage caches only the current local world-grid window. Animated light strength is recombined every frame; walking to another grid cell or changing light geometry invalidates coverage, and old entries are removed.
- Exterior presentation is limited to 60 draws/sec even on 120/144 Hz displays; simulation/input retains its elapsed time. Hidden background-play continues simulation but skips graphics. A paused, unchanged scene skips GPU work, while explicit refresh, resize and input-triggered redraw remain available.

`test-outside-render-budget-ui.cjs` checks 30/60/120/144 Hz simulation versus draw counts, paused/hidden work, explicit refresh, DPR 2 physical resolution, off-screen uploads, scissor restoration and grid/direct fog equivalence. `test-character-shadows.cjs` compares cached fill to independent polygon evaluation under movement and changing light power. Existing motion/optical tests continue checking bloom flicker, sprite-frame reflections, multi-light shadow strength and receiver clipping.

Validation for this optimization: smoke **16/16**, outside **52/52**, structure/site checks and whitespace checks passed. The reusable profiler ran successfully with hardware GPU timers and no page errors. No engine source, original images, rain simulation settings or gameplay rules were changed.

## Continuous exterior bloom (2026-10-27)

The first-commute doorway revealed a spatial defect not covered by the earlier total-energy test: the five-tap blur expanded its sample offsets with DPR and spread. At high resolution, adjacent samples no longer covered adjacent texels. Bright vertical/horizontal edges therefore produced repeated stripes and a crosshatch around the doorway/arrow. A three-pixel cyan line reproduced 11 outward brightness rises at physical widths 2560 and 3840; total light energy could remain stable despite these visible bands.

Bloom now integrates every neighboring texel with a normalized Gaussian. Bilinear taps combine adjacent pairs using their exact weighted offset; radius changes the weights/support, not the spacing. A bounded light-only buffer (at most 320 pixels wide) keeps this dense filter affordable. Original scene extraction still thresholds each texel of the full 4×4 physical footprint before reduction, followed by repeated footprint-averaged half-size passes and two separable blur passes. The full-resolution scene is composited only at the end. The Gaussian variance approximates the previous two-scale spread, preserving the authored intensity/threshold and leaving sprite art sharp. The prior scissors, native-cell fog, 60 Hz draw budget and paused/hidden suppression remain unchanged.

`test-street-bloom-continuity-ui.cjs` checks monotonic fading on both axes, four one-pixel source phases and widths 800/1280/1920/2560/3840. It also checks light-energy stability and captures the real first-commute doorway before/after camera movement. `BLOOM_BASELINE=/path/to/saved-renderer.js` optionally verifies that the new regression fails against the old shader; that failure was reproduced before validating the fix. All 40 new profiles have zero outward brightness rises. The existing motion test continues checking live reflection, moving thin lights and paused frames.

Paired local GPU-timer runs (Chrome/Metal on Apple M4 Pro, 2560×1440 physical rainy street, 60 sampled frames each) measured the complete bloom pass at mean 1.96/2.33 ms before and 1.03/1.25 ms after. These are local pass timings, not OS utilization claims. The bounded blur adds no full-resolution scene blur; the earlier exterior render budget remains in force.

Validation: smoke **16/16** and outside **53/53** passed, including structure/site checks, the new continuity regression, moving-light/reflection tests and the prior 60 Hz/hidden/paused rendering-budget checks. Real doorway and adjacent neon screenshots were inspected before and after, including a shifted camera. No original art, gameplay or weather settings changed.

## Exterior-wide soft-light follow-up (2026-10-27)

The continuous bloom above applies to the complete exterior render, including signs, lamps and reflected light. A follow-up audit covered the lift, alley, shops, bar, restaurant and upper apartment corridor, plus dry shop/bar views. Sign-cookie and wire-spark halos already use smooth scaling separately from their pixel-art cores. Two other soft fields still used nearest-neighbor enlargement:

- Fog compositing selected one native density cell with `texelFetch`. It now interpolates only the cached density field, with a guard cell around the viewport to prevent clamping when the camera crosses a world-cell boundary. The very-wide-view fallback evaluates continuous world coordinates. Scene colors still come from the full-resolution source; building and character pixels are not blurred.
- Ordinary city light cookies inherited the scene's nearest-neighbor sampling, including rotated upward beams. The sprite draw scopes smooth filtering to these light-only screen-blended images. Neon keeps its existing separate smooth halo and sharp core; ordinary sprites retain nearest-neighbor sampling.

`test-street-soft-light-ui.cjs` exercises a steep fog ramp, cell-boundary camera positions and eight real scene/weather views while moving left/right. The pre-fix renderer fails the ramp with a maximum adjacent-pixel jump of 16 channel levels; the corrected renderer reaches 2. `SOFT_LIGHT_BASELINE` can replay that failure. The existing rendering-budget test still verifies 60 Hz pacing, paused/hidden suppression, native resolution and off-screen culling; interpolated grid versus continuous direct fog differs by at most one channel level at the three tested camera offsets. Source artwork, imported atmosphere presets, shadows and rain simulation are unchanged.

A local GPU-timer check at 2560×1440 physical resolution measured one fog layer at mean 0.77 ms before and 0.88 ms after interpolation (180 layer samples per run). The three layers therefore added about 0.33 ms in this single comparison; CPU-submission medians were 2.5/2.3 ms and both runs maintained ~16.7 ms rAF intervals. This is a small measured quality cost, not a claim that smoothing is free or that all devices behave identically; no extra full-scene blur pass was added.

Follow-up validation: smoke **16/16** and outside **54/54** passed, including both new continuity regressions. Structure/site and whitespace checks passed.
